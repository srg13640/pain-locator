The 3D body in this app is not something I drew from boxes and spheres.

It comes from Z-Anatomy, an atlas made by Gauthier Kervyn and packaged for a desktop viewer by Lluís Vinent Juanico.
Z-Anatomy is licensed Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International.
That means you may use and share it if you credit the authors and share changes to the models under the same license.

Z-Anatomy is derived from BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, Research Organization of Information and Systems, Japan.
The polygon files in BodyParts3D release 4.0 carry this notice: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 2.1 Japan.
The current BodyParts3D database readme states Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International.
Credit both projects if you share these models.

Suggested credit line:
"Z-Anatomy (Gauthier Kervyn; Lluís Vinent Juanico), CC BY-SA 4.0, derived from BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science."

Foundational Model of Anatomy (FMA) numbers are matched from the BodyParts3D name table when the mesh name agrees with that table.
FMA is maintained by the University of Washington.

The woman's outer surface is a separate model: VH_F_Skin from the HuBMAP Common Coordinate Framework 3D reference library, version 1.2.
It was derived from the Visible Human Female, National Library of Medicine, for the Human Reference Atlas.
Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein are among the people who built that reference skin.
The library is Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International.
https://humanatlas.io/3d-reference-library
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

This app lines that woman's skin up with the atlas (height, feet, and the front of the breastbone, with the patient's left on +X) and divides it into the atlas skin regions by place.
The bones, muscles, and organs behind it are still the Z-Anatomy atlas. They are not a second, female reconstruction of each organ.
